# Minimum Validation Experiment Library

Choose the experiment for the highest-impact assumption with the weakest
evidence. Do not default to a landing page because it is easy to generate.

## Experiment contract

Define before execution:

```markdown
- Decision this test informs:
- Hypothesis:
- Current evidence level:
- Representative participant / source:
- Method and artifact:
- Exposure denominator:
- Timebox:
- Founder hours / cash at risk:
- Pass threshold:
- Inconclusive rule:
- Fail threshold:
- Decision after pass / inconclusive / fail:
- Bias and contamination risks:
```

Thresholds must reflect base rates, traffic quality, price, and decision size.
Never invent one universal conversion threshold for every business.

## Problem discovery interview

Best for: E0–E1 problem, urgency, workflow, or buyer hypotheses.

Method:

- recruit representative people with a recent relevant trigger
- ask about the last actual event, current workaround, consequence, effort,
  decision process, and money already spent
- do not pitch the solution during problem discovery

Signal: repeated behavior and consequence, not compliments or hypothetical use.

## Workflow observation or artifact review

Best for: operational pain that people struggle to describe accurately.

Method: with permission, observe the task or review redacted examples of the
current workflow. Record steps, delays, handoffs, errors, and workarounds.

Signal: repeated measurable friction in the claimed critical step.

## Fixed-scope paid diagnostic

Best for: expertise-led B2B services, audits, benchmarks, and decision support.

Method: sell a narrow outcome with explicit scope, price, buyer, delivery time,
and exclusions. Deliver manually to learn before automating.

Signal: qualified payment plus usefulness of the delivered decision artifact.

Risk: confusing discounts, friend purchases, or consulting favors with repeatable demand.

## Concierge pilot

Best for: testing the result and workflow before building software.

Method: manually deliver the promised outcome behind a simple interface or
service boundary. Track founder time, exceptions, quality, and repeat use.

Signal: buyer commitment, successful outcome, repeat behavior, and manageable delivery load.

## Pre-sale or paid reservation

Best for: testing willingness to pay when the scope and delivery date can be
stated honestly.

Method: offer the exact future deliverable, price, refund terms, capacity, and
delivery conditions. Do not imply a product already exists.

Signal: qualified payment under transparent terms.

## Letter of intent or design-partner agreement

Best for: enterprise or high-ticket offers where procurement prevents immediate payment.

Method: ask a real decision-maker to commit named time, data, access, or a buying
process milestone in writing.

Signal: costly organizational action. A nonbinding compliment is not commitment.

## Smoke or fake-door test

Best for: comparing messages or measuring qualified action before a build.

Method: clearly represent availability and next step; measure exposure to the
right audience, CTA actions, qualification, and follow-through.

Signal: completed qualified behavior. A click or waitlist alone does not prove willingness to pay.

Safety: do not mislead users, collect unnecessary data, or claim unavailable functionality.

## Prototype task test

Best for: usability, comprehension, and workflow risk after the problem is credible.

Method: ask representative users to complete realistic tasks with a low-fidelity
prototype. Observe behavior without coaching.

Signal: task completion, errors, time, and comprehension; not general aesthetic preference.

## Manual channel test

Best for: reachability and buyer-conversation assumptions.

Method: contact a bounded number of representative prospects through a lawful,
relevant, personalized route. Track delivered exposure, replies, qualification,
conversations, and commitments separately.

Signal: qualified progression. Do not use spam volume to compensate for weak targeting.

## Price test

Best for: a credible problem and offer with uncertain pricing.

Method: present real buying conditions to comparable qualified buyers. Change
one material variable at a time when possible and include delivery cost.

Signal: purchase or costly next step at the stated terms; stated preference is weak evidence.

## Evidence strength preference

When cost and safety are reasonable, prefer:

1. paid or costly commitment
2. repeated real use or renewal
3. completed buyer behavior
4. observed workaround and consequence
5. past-behavior interview
6. stated preference, attention, click, or waitlist

The cheapest experiment is not always the smallest useful experiment. Choose the
cheapest one capable of changing the decision.
