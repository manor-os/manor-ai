# Fictional Example — Solo Business Idea Review

This example is fictional. All people, prices, and signals below are invented for
the example input and must never be presented as real market evidence.

## Example input

**Idea:** Sell a $650 fixed-scope client-onboarding audit to boutique marketing
agencies, then decide whether repeated delivery justifies templates, a benchmark,
or software.

**Founder evidence supplied:**

- six years of agency operations experience
- fourteen warm agency-owner contacts
- four recent interviews; all four described delayed kickoff or repeated missing-input workarounds
- two owners previously paid the founder for adjacent operational help, not this exact audit
- eight hours/week available and a $600 test budget
- no software build and no client-credential handling before paid demand

## Executive verdict

- **Verdict:** PROCEED TO TEST
- **Score:** 76 / 100 — comparison aid, not a 76% success probability
- **Confidence:** Medium-low
- **Judgment:** Founder fit, buyer access, and manual testability justify a bounded
  paid-offer test; willingness to pay for a diagnostic and sustainable delivery
  load remain unvalidated.
- **Strongest evidence for:** repeated recent workaround reports from a relevant
  warm network plus adjacent paid trust
- **Strongest evidence against:** no one has paid for the exact audit; all four
  interviews may be biased by the founder's network
- **What could reverse it:** no buying conversations after qualified exposure, or
  delivery taking more than four founder hours per audit

## Twelve-month pre-mortem

1. Owners expect the audit to be free sales discovery, so conversations do not
   convert.
   - Early warning: detailed interest followed by refusal to pay for diagnosis.
   - Cheap test: show fixed scope and $650 price before scheduling diagnostic work.
2. Every agency workflow is different, and delivery grows beyond eight hours.
   - Early warning: more than two unplanned data requests or custom artifacts per pilot.
   - Cheap test: time each step and record exceptions across the first two paid audits.
3. The founder builds software from two similar cases and discovers that process
   ownership, not tooling, caused the delays.
   - Early warning: recommendations are managerial rather than workflow/tool changes.
   - Mitigation: prohibit software work until at least five paid deliveries reveal
     the same automatable bottleneck.

## Next decisive experiment

- **Decision:** whether to run two paid audit pilots
- **Hypothesis:** at least two of ten qualified warm agency owners will buy or
  enter a concrete buying conversation for the exact $650 fixed-scope audit
- **Method:** personalized outreach with one-page scope, price, exclusions, and
  two available delivery slots
- **Timebox:** seven days; ten qualified delivered exposures
- **Risk:** maximum five founder hours before payment; no ad spend
- **Pass:** two payments at the stated price
- **Inconclusive:** fewer than eight qualified recipients see the offer, or two
  enter buying conversations delayed by a documented procurement/timing issue
- **Fail:** eight or more qualified exposures and no buying conversation, or all
  relevant objections reject paying for diagnosis
- **After pass:** deliver two pilots, measure hours/outcome, then review repeatability
- **After inconclusive:** repair exposure/sample once without changing price and scope simultaneously
- **After fail:** reframe as an implementation outcome or stop; do not build software
- **Approval:** outreach is an external action and requires the user's approval of
  the exact message, recipients, and send plan
