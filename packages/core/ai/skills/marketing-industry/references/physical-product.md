# Physical product, ecommerce, and equipment marketing

Identify exact product, model/SKU, variant, buyer, use context, purchase channel, price/availability source, and fulfillment region. Product identity must stay consistent between creative, listing, cart, and delivered item. For B2B equipment, include operator, purchasing role, installation/training/service constraints, and a qualified demo or spec-request path.

| Buyer question | Proof to collect | Creative use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is this the correct item? | Real photos, dimensions, color/material, model/variant | Show exact silhouette, scale and labels |
| Does it perform as claimed? | Demonstration, test method, compatible inputs, limits | Show the real workflow and result, not synthetic evidence |
| Can I obtain and use it? | Price, inventory, delivery, warranty/service, returns when stated | Align copy with product page and fulfillment |
| Can my team adopt it? (B2B) | Installation, training, throughput/ROI only when substantiated | Lead with operator workflow and spec handoff |

Write a claim ledger for features, origin, materials, certifications, performance, savings, testimonials, and comparative claims. Use the exact source or `[verification needed]`; omit unsupported specifics from public-facing copy. If generated visuals are needed, label concepts and keep the real product's geometry, controls, packaging, and use sequence faithful. Avoid invented product interfaces or machine outputs.

Choose a CTA that fits the sales cycle: product detail, add to cart, retailer, distributor inquiry, sample, spec sheet, or demo. Measure completed purchase or qualified lead, plus returns/lead quality when available. Product-page visits alone are an intermediate signal.
