# Services, hospitality, education, and unfamiliar sectors

For a sector not covered by the dedicated product or restaurant playbooks, derive the message from five facts: **buyer problem or occasion**, **service or experience actually delivered**, **evidence of capability**, **constraints on who/where/when it is available**, and **the next safe action**. Do not borrow a restaurant or SaaS pattern just because it is familiar.

| Sector | Critical buyer question | Evidence and CTA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local professional/home service | Can this provider solve my nearby issue reliably? | Verified service area, qualifications, process and availability; call, quote or appointment |
| Hospitality/travel/event | Is the place/date/experience a fit? | Actual property/venue, inclusions, capacity, booking terms; availability check or inquiry |
| Education/creator product | Will this teach the desired skill? | Real curriculum/sample, instructor background, prerequisites and outcome limits; sample or enrollment |
| Health/finance/legal/regulated | Is this claim permitted and appropriate here? | Approved source claims, jurisdiction and required disclosures; compliant inquiry or consultation |

For regulated or high-stakes claims, verify current jurisdiction-specific requirements through authoritative sources before publishing. Treat missing approval or evidence as a claim blocker, not a reason to invent a softer factual assertion. Keep an educational draft clearly separated from personalized professional advice.

Return the buyer question, facts available, claim gaps, offer, proof asset, CTA, and measurable business event. When the sector is unfamiliar, say which assumptions need domain review before release.
