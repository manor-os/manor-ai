# Founder Inventory

Use this reference to turn the founder's starting position into idea constraints.
Do not force every user through a long questionnaire. Extract what is already
known, ask one high-value question when needed, and label the rest `Unknown`.

## Minimum viable intake

Capture these fields before ranking ideas:

| Field | What to record | Why it changes the ideas |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome | Monthly income goal, learning goal, asset goal, or growth ambition | A fast cash-flow service and a long-lived software asset optimize for different outcomes |
| Capacity | Weekly hours, energy pattern, synchronous availability | Rules out support-heavy or meeting-heavy models |
| Test budget | Cash available before evidence | Prevents capital-intensive recommendations |
| Cash deadline | When the project must show a meaningful signal or revenue | Determines acceptable sales and build cycle |
| Risk boundary | Income, reputation, legal, privacy, safety, relationship, and platform risk | Hard filter, not a scoring bonus |
| Exclusions | Industries, tasks, customers, channels, or business forms the founder will not use | Avoids attractive but unusable directions |

## Operator advantage inventory

### Hard-won insight

- problems the founder has repeatedly solved
- processes they understand from the inside
- mistakes, transitions, or transformations they have personally experienced
- unusual combinations of domain and functional knowledge

Ask: `What do people with less experience consistently misunderstand here?`

### Customer access

- people the founder can contact directly and ethically
- communities where they are a legitimate participant
- former customers, colleagues, partners, or audience segments
- buyer roles whose language and decision process they understand

Record a realistic count. “I know the industry” is not the same as access to ten
representative buyers.

### Credibility and proof

- measurable outcomes, case studies, portfolio, certifications, trusted roles
- examples of delivered work
- public teaching or writing that demonstrates expertise
- referrals and relationships that can transfer trust

Do not turn confidential client work into public proof without permission.

### Reusable assets

- templates, checklists, datasets, scripts, code, calculators, research, content
- repeatable methods and delivery steps
- licenses or rights to use and commercialize the asset
- existing product, audience, domain, distribution, or operational infrastructure

Confirm ownership and permitted use. An employer's or client's material is not a
founder asset merely because the founder created it at work.

### Distribution

- email list, social audience, search traffic, partnerships, community standing
- repeatable outbound route and realistic response history
- marketplaces or platforms already used successfully
- owned versus rented channels

Record audience relevance and engagement, not just follower count.

### Delivery capability

- work the founder can perform at professional quality now
- tasks that can be standardized, delegated, automated, or supported by AI
- tasks requiring a credential, specialist, supplier, or team
- realistic support and quality-control capacity

### Constraints and hard boundaries

- time zones, caregiving, health, employment agreements, conflicts of interest
- privacy, safety, reputation, or regulated-service exposure
- aversion to sales calls, public identity, travel, inventory, or recurring support
- maximum acceptable loss and stop date

Treat these as design inputs. Do not “motivate” the founder past a hard boundary.

## Evidence tags

Tag inventory entries:

- **Provided fact** — directly stated by the founder or present in a selected record
- **Observed evidence** — artifact, behavior, result, or dated record supports it
- **Inference** — reasonable interpretation that still needs confirmation
- **Assumption** — planning placeholder
- **Unknown** — not available

Ideas may use assumptions, but the ranking must not hide them.
