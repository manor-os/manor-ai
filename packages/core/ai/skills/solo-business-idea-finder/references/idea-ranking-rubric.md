# Idea Ranking Rubric

Rank ideas only after hard constraints are applied. The score compares candidates
under current assumptions; it is not a probability of success or proof of demand.

## Hard filters

Mark each `Pass`, `Fail`, or `Specialist review required`:

- compatible with founder's weekly capacity and cash deadline
- testable within the stated loss limit
- no known ethical or safety conflict
- no obvious credential or legal barrier the founder cannot satisfy
- at least one plausible route to representative buyers
- no unavoidable capital, inventory, or delivery burden beyond the founder's limit
- founder has rights to any required data, content, code, or customer information

Do not score a failed hard filter as if strengths elsewhere offset it.

## Evidence levels

- **E0 — Unsupported:** assumption or analogy only
- **E1 — Indication:** one relevant report, request, conversation, or artifact
- **E2 — Repeated signal:** multiple independent relevant observations or records
- **E3 — Behavior:** buyer spends meaningful time, money, effort, or accepts risk on the problem
- **E4 — Commitment:** payment, pre-order, signed pilot, repeated usage, renewal, or equivalent costly commitment

Record evidence level per dimension. Do not assign the entire idea one evidence
level if different claims have different support.

## Eight dimensions

Score each 0 to 5.

### 1. Problem evidence

- 0: problem is invented or contradicted
- 1: plausible but unsupported
- 2: isolated relevant statements
- 3: repeated pain or observed workaround
- 4: material current cost or active solution search
- 5: repeated costly behavior or commitment tied to the problem

### 2. Founder advantage and trust

- 0: no capability, credibility, access, or learning path
- 1: generic interest only
- 2: adjacent capability
- 3: relevant delivery experience or credible proof
- 4: uncommon insight plus proof and relationships
- 5: strong trust, proprietary/owned asset, or repeatable demonstrated outcome

### 3. Buyer reachability

- 0: no identifiable buyer or lawful route
- 1: broad cold audience only
- 2: a plausible channel but no access proof
- 3: at least ten representative prospects can be contacted
- 4: recurring channel or warm network with relevant engagement
- 5: owned/repeatable access already produces buyer conversations

### 4. Willingness-to-pay signal

- 0: no payer or economic value
- 1: founder-selected price only
- 2: comparable alternatives or stated interest
- 3: buyer already pays or incurs measurable cost for alternatives
- 4: relevant price discussion, LOI, deposit, or paid adjacent work
- 5: repeated payment, pre-order, paid pilot, or renewal for this outcome

### 5. Speed to first credible signal or revenue

- 0: more than six months or major capital before learning
- 1: long build or approval cycle
- 2: one to three months
- 3: two to four weeks
- 4: one week with existing access/assets
- 5: a credible buyer test can start within 48 hours

### 6. Solo operability

- 0: requires a team, credential, inventory, or constant support
- 1: delivery grows nearly one-for-one with customers
- 2: heavy custom work or synchronous dependency
- 3: bounded service with manageable exceptions
- 4: standardized delivery and support with automation leverage
- 5: low marginal workload, clear quality controls, and resilient operations

### 7. Repeatability and leverage

- 0: no reusable output or process
- 1: bespoke work dominates
- 2: some reusable templates or content
- 3: repeatable method with productization path
- 4: most value comes from owned reusable assets or automation
- 5: compounding data, distribution, content, code, or workflow assets improve the offer

### 8. Dependency and downside resilience

- 0: one external change can end the business and loss is material
- 1: severe platform, supplier, model, regulatory, or customer concentration
- 2: major dependency with weak fallback
- 3: dependency is visible and partially mitigated
- 4: portable assets, multiple routes, bounded downside
- 5: low fixed cost, reversible tests, owned relationships, and credible fallbacks

## Evidence caps

Apply these caps even when the idea sounds compelling:

- no customer/problem evidence: Problem evidence maximum 1
- only stated preference: Willingness-to-pay maximum 2
- no named route to ten prospects: Buyer reachability maximum 2
- unknown delivery hours at ten customers: Solo operability maximum 2
- unknown source/IP rights: Repeatability maximum 2 and flag the hard filter
- idea depends on one platform with no fallback: Resilience maximum 2

## Portfolio score

Add the eight scores for a total out of 40. Interpret only after the hard filters
and evidence levels are visible:

- **31–40:** high comparative fit; proceed to a bounded evidence test
- **23–30:** promising but ranking depends on named unknowns
- **15–22:** weak current fit; reframe before testing
- **0–14:** do not prioritize under current constraints

An E0-heavy idea cannot be described as high-confidence regardless of total.
Show which two new facts would most change the ranking.
