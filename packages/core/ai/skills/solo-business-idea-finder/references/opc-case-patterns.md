# One-Person Business Case Patterns

Use this reference to distinguish a genuinely solo-operable business pattern
from a generic small-business idea. The cases below are pattern evidence, not
proof that a newly derived niche has demand.

## What the upstream OPC Skills contain

The `easychen/opc-methodology` repository was rechecked at commit
`b3d0503a52298a2fbe4751231f3119d6a015eab5`. Its `skills/` directory contains
resource audit, niche positioning, value proposition, business-model, MVP,
conversion, asset, review, and orchestration workflows. It does not contain a
prewritten business-idea database.

The accompanying book source discusses business categories and cases, but it
is licensed CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0. Do not copy its prose, examples, templates, or
classification tables into Manor. Use the repository only to check coverage and
to understand that code leverage, media leverage, changing market boundaries,
founder resources, standardization, and cash flow all matter.

## Reusable patterns from independently reviewed cases

| Pattern | Public case signal | Transferable mechanism | Solo-operator watchout |
|---|---|---|---|
| Browser-local utility | Photopea grew as a browser editor with unusually low server cost and ad/API revenue | Move costly processing to the user's device; monetize premium workflows, licensing, or ads | A broad editor takes years; start with one painful file or batch task |
| Vertical micro-SaaS | Formula Bot converted one narrow spreadsheet job into a self-serve product; Zigpoll focused on an e-commerce feedback moment | Make one frequent job obvious, repeatable, and self-serve | Generic AI wrappers are easy to copy; retain workflow context or distribution advantage |
| Platform marketplace add-on | Amit Agarwal built a portfolio of Google Workspace add-ons; Zigpoll used the Shopify ecosystem | Borrow an existing platform's identity, data model, billing context, and discovery | Platform policy and ranking changes can become a single point of failure |
| Open core plus paid features | Sidekiq paired a useful open-source core with paid Pro and Enterprise capabilities | Let free usage create trust and distribution, then charge for high-value operational features | Open-source support can overwhelm one maintainer; publish a strict support boundary |
| One-purpose API | ScreenshotOne packaged a difficult browser capability behind simple usage pricing | Sell a reliable primitive to developers instead of a complete end-user workflow | Reliability, abuse, and infrastructure spend can erase the apparent leverage |
| Browser extension wedge | Superpower ChatGPT began as a small extension around a fast-growing platform, then added subscriptions and media | Enter through one missing in-context action, then expand into a standalone asset | DOM changes and store rules create maintenance and concentration risk |
| Self-serve mobile subscription | Rootd and GymStreak show how a focused consumer outcome can be delivered repeatedly through an app | Turn a recurring personal routine into guided, on-demand software | Acquisition and content quality matter more than feature count; regulated claims require review |
| Directory or data product | Nomad List, Remote OK, and specialist comparison sites package fragmented information for a narrow audience | Normalize public or contributed data, then monetize access, listings, sponsorships, or leads | Data freshness is the product; manual curation can quietly become a full-time job |
| Paid niche media | Founder newsletters and membership publications combine a recurring point of view with subscriptions, sponsors, or products | Publish once to many, own the audience, and layer monetization paths | A newsletter is not automatically a business; distribution and a costly information gap are prerequisites |
| Digital template or asset shop | Easlo and theme creators sell reusable systems and assets with near-zero marginal fulfillment | Encode hard-won judgment into a reusable template, component, theme, or asset pack | Generic templates are easy to produce and hard to sell without audience or ecosystem discovery |
| Productized subscription service | Jim Designs and DesignJoy standardize scope, queue, turnaround, and price | Use a service for fast cash flow while constraining variability like a product | Capacity remains finite; define one-at-a-time delivery, pauses, and a client ceiling |
| Expert knowledge product | Creator businesses combine focused content, courses, books, tools, and communities | Convert repeated expertise into a curriculum and reusable assets rather than hourly advice | Expertise and trust must predate the product; avoid credentialed or safety-critical advice without review |
| Micro-product portfolio | Pieter Levels and Marc Lou operate multiple small products sharing audience, infrastructure, or launch skill | Reuse one distribution channel and technical base across several narrow bets | A portfolio without shared distribution becomes many support burdens instead of diversification |

## Use rules

1. Use a case only to establish that a delivery and revenue pattern can be run
   by one person. Never transfer its reported revenue to a new idea.
2. Derive new candidates by changing the buyer, triggering job, and founder
   advantage. Do not recommend cloning the named product.
3. Prefer code, media, data, or reusable-asset leverage. Allow services only
   when scope, queue, price, and client ceiling are standardized.
4. Penalize platform concentration, support load, data-refresh labor,
   infrastructure cost, regulated claims, and audience dependence.
5. Label every starter as a hypothesis until direct buyer behavior supplies
   evidence.
