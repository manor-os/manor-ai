# Idea Generation Patterns

Use patterns to diversify candidates, not to manufacture unsupported demand.
Every idea must still name the buyer, trigger, current alternative, founder fit,
operating load, and first test.

## 1. Repeated-work productization

Turn a recurring expert task into a fixed-scope diagnostic, implementation, or
maintenance package.

Strong inputs:

- repeated request from similar buyers
- stable steps and deliverables
- outcome that can be scoped and priced
- examples of the founder already doing the work

Primary risk: hidden customization makes every engagement a new job.

## 2. Painful workaround replacement

Replace a spreadsheet, manual reconciliation, copy-paste workflow, recurring
search, or coordination burden with a service, automation, or narrow tool.

Strong inputs:

- people already spend time, money, or risk to maintain the workaround
- trigger and frequency are observable
- one narrow step creates most of the value

Primary risk: the workaround is cheap and good enough.

## 3. Paid diagnostic before implementation

Sell an audit, benchmark, assessment, roadmap, or decision memo that creates
value before a larger service or product exists.

Strong inputs:

- expensive decision with unclear options
- founder credibility and repeatable evaluation method
- customer can act on the output independently

Primary risk: diagnostic becomes disguised unpaid sales work or requires a
regulated professional judgment.

## 4. Service-to-system ladder

Start with a manual or concierge service, then convert repeated delivery into
templates, training, workflow automation, data, or software.

Strong inputs:

- direct customer access
- willingness to perform the work manually to learn
- recurring patterns that can become assets

Primary risk: assuming productization before enough repeated delivery exists.

## 5. Audience-to-offer fit

Build a narrow paid outcome for an audience the founder already reaches.

Strong inputs:

- repeated questions or actions from a relevant audience
- a specific moment when the audience needs help
- channel access that is not purely algorithmic

Primary risk: mistaking attention, entertainment, or peer interest for buyer intent.

## 6. Decision-support product

Package recurring research, monitoring, comparison, curation, or analysis into a
brief, alert, database, calculator, or subscription.

Strong inputs:

- decision recurs and has a consequence
- reliable, licensable inputs
- customer values timeliness or reduced uncertainty

Primary risk: source licensing, data freshness, weak differentiation, or low
renewal value.

## 7. Asset derivative

Turn legitimately owned methods, examples, templates, code, content, or datasets
created during prior work into a separate offer.

Strong inputs:

- clear ownership and commercialization rights
- asset already saves time or improves quality
- identifiable customer with the same job

Primary risk: employer/client IP, confidentiality, or an asset without an urgent use.

## 8. Capability bridge

Help a narrow customer adopt a new technology, regulation, channel, or workflow
using implementation, migration, quality assurance, or training.

Strong inputs:

- recent change creates a real deadline or cost difference
- buyers lack a specific capability
- founder can deliver without claiming credentials they do not have

Primary risk: temporary demand, vendor dependence, or commoditization after the
transition becomes routine.

## 9. Niche operations layer

Offer a small business function that a niche needs but cannot justify hiring for:
reporting, onboarding, quality control, documentation, customer education, or
specialized administration.

Strong inputs:

- recurring need across similar firms
- service level can be standardized
- communication and exception handling stay bounded

Primary risk: support load grows linearly and turns the solo business into a job.

## 10. Marketplace or platform complement

Build tools, data, education, or services around an existing ecosystem.

Strong inputs:

- clear user workflow and distribution inside or adjacent to the ecosystem
- value survives reasonable API, fee, or policy changes
- a path toward owned customer relationships or portable assets

Primary risk: platform concentration, terms changes, or loss of access.

## Variation operators

Apply only when the change creates a meaningfully different business:

- narrower buyer with a more urgent trigger
- manual-first versus software-first delivery
- one-time outcome versus recurring monitoring
- premium done-for-you versus self-serve asset
- horizontal workflow versus industry-specific version
- direct sale versus channel partner
- local/regional constraint versus global digital delivery

Discard cosmetic variations that share the same customer, problem, offer, and
risk. Diversity is useful only when it changes the decision.
