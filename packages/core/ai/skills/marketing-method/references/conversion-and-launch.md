# Conversion work, launches, events, and promotions

Use conversion work when there is traffic or interest but a weak path to the desired action. Use a launch/event/promotion when a real date, release, opening, offer, or capacity creates a timely reason to act. These can combine, but do not invent urgency.

## Conversion path

Map visitor question → message/offer → proof → friction → CTA → completed event. Inspect the actual page, form, booking, product listing, or checkout before claiming a problem. Prioritize one fix that removes a known obstacle, such as a missing booking link, unclear price source, unreadable mobile proof, or a mismatch between ad and landing promise. If testing, define hypothesis, one principal change, eligibility/traffic, primary metric, guardrail, and decision rule. If sample size is too small for a trustworthy comparison, use qualitative observations and label uncertainty.

## Launch sequence

Confirm what is launching, exact date/time/timezone, geography, inventory/capacity, eligibility, offer terms, destination, owner, and rollback/correction contact. Create a sequence appropriate to the goal: prelaunch education or waitlist only if real, announcement with proof, day-of availability and CTA, follow-up to responders, and post-launch measurement. A restaurant opening needs location/hours/menu readiness; a product release needs a shipped feature and accurate demo; an event needs attendance and booking logistics.

Deliver the requested landing copy, announcement, campaign calendar, offer block, or experiment brief with exact status. Measure the closest action: booking, order, signup/activation, qualified lead, attendance, or purchase. Do not call a planned launch live or promise a conversion uplift without observed evidence.
