# Search, SEO, and local discovery

Use when people already express a product, service, or location need. Separate technical/site SEO, useful content matching query intent, and local listing accuracy. A real audit needs access to the site/listing and current evidence; without it, provide an explicitly prospective plan.

1. Group queries by intent: learn, compare, buy/book, or visit nearby. Do not fabricate search volume or ranking position.
2. Map each group to an existing or proposed page/listing. Identify the buyer question, answer, proof, CTA, and next page/action. Avoid creating multiple thin pages for near-identical intents.
3. For local businesses, verify name, address/service area, hours, categories, phone, menu/services, reservation/order link, photos, and offer terms across relevant listings. Fix wrong operational data before producing new posts.
4. For products, align category/use-case pages and exact product pages with real features, variants, pricing source, availability, and checkout/demo path.
5. Measure qualified search visits, calls, directions, bookings, leads, or purchases. Record baseline only from accessible analytics; attribution from maps or offline visits may be incomplete.

Deliver a prioritized worklist with issue/evidence, suggested change, destination, responsible asset, expected buyer effect as a hypothesis, and validation step. `seo-audit` can be invoked by the parent campaign if installed and an actual website audit is requested. Do not promise rankings or infer that a listing update instantly changes discovery.
